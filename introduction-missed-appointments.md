# Introduction: No-Show Appointments in Outpatient Physical Therapy

## Revised text

Non-attendance at scheduled visits is a persistent problem in outpatient healthcare. Each unused slot represents lost clinical capacity, lengthens waiting lists, and delays care for other patients. These consequences are particularly relevant to rehabilitation, where patients typically need a course of regular sessions spread over several weeks to achieve their treatment goals.

Evidence from Saudi Arabia on this issue is still emerging. A recent study conducted in the Adult Rehabilitation Department at King Abdulaziz Medical City, Riyadh, found that about one in five scheduled appointments (approximately 21%) ended in a no-show. When the researchers followed up with the patients concerned, the most common explanations were illness on the day of the visit, difficulty getting to the clinic, inconvenient appointment times, job commitments, and various other personal reasons. The authors also noted that few Saudi studies have examined missed appointments in rehabilitation settings.

Because the factors behind non-attendance can differ from one facility and region to another, findings from Riyadh cannot simply be assumed to apply elsewhere. King Salman Hospital in Taif receives patients both from the city and from the surrounding areas, so evidence gathered within the hospital itself is needed to plan effective attendance strategies. At present, no systematic study has measured how often patients miss appointments at the hospital's outpatient physical therapy clinics or why they do so.

This study therefore aims to determine the rate of no-show appointments in outpatient physical therapy at King Salman Hospital and to identify the reasons patients give for missing their sessions. The findings are expected to give the department a better picture of non-attendance and to guide future improvements in appointment scheduling, reminder systems, patient communication, and access to physical therapy services.
