# Introduction: No-Show Appointments in Outpatient Physical Therapy

## Paraphrased text

Failure to attend booked appointments continues to be a widespread challenge for outpatient services. When patients do not arrive, the reserved time goes unused, the clinic's output declines, waiting lists grow longer, and other patients must wait longer to be seen. Rehabilitation services are especially vulnerable to these effects, since therapy usually relies on a sequence of sessions attended regularly over a period of several weeks.

Within Saudi Arabia, a recent investigation carried out in the Adult Rehabilitation Department of King Abdulaziz Medical City in Riyadh reported that roughly 21% of booked appointments were not attended during the data collection period. The researchers telephoned patients who had not attended in order to learn why. The reasons most often given were sickness on the day of the session, lack of transport, appointment times that did not suit the patient, job commitments, and a range of other personal factors. The authors further pointed out that national research on non-attendance in rehabilitation remains scarce.

The causes of missed physiotherapy sessions are unlikely to be the same in every hospital or region. King Salman Hospital in Taif provides care to residents of Taif and of the neighbouring communities, which makes evidence drawn from its own patient population essential for developing suitable measures to improve attendance. So far, no formal study has determined either how frequently appointments are missed in the hospital's outpatient physical therapy clinics or what reasons patients give for missing them.

For this reason, the present study aims to measure the proportion of missed appointments in outpatient physical therapy and to explore the key reasons patients report for not attending. Its findings may help the department better understand how and why appointments are missed, and may support future efforts to improve scheduling, appointment reminders, patient communication, and access to physiotherapy care.
